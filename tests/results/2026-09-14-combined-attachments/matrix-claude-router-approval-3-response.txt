Stop on rejection. Steps, reviewer, and policy are supplied, and no model interprets the form or picks the route.

**3. Responsibilities.** Not atomic. Four responsibilities:

- **R1** Validate the submitted typed form against the supplied rules.
- **R2** Obtain and verify the named reviewer's recorded approval, or stop on rejection.
- **R3** Invoke the authoritative service to apply the change, then retain its audit result.
- **R4** Sequence R1 to R3, hold state across the human wait, and terminate on rejection.

**4. Routing decisions.**

- **R1: deterministic code.** A typed form with supplied rules is a fixed validation. The requirement forbids model interpretation, so nothing above rung one is permitted.
- **R2: deterministic code.** Match the recorded decision to the named reviewer's identity and the supplied policy. Rejection is a fixed branch. No content judgement is involved.
- **R3: deterministic code.** One fixed call to a named service, followed by persisting a returned record.
- **R4: workflow, on the orthogonal axis.** Fixed sequence, one durable wait state, one terminal branch. This needs persisted process control because the wait spans human time, but it needs no autonomy.

**5. Bounded-AI challenge record.** No responsibility was routed to an agent, so the challenge was not needed to permit one. It was still run for R1 and R2 to confirm the routing. Could a single fixed-shape call satisfy either? Yes in principle, but deterministic code already satisfies both fully, and the requirement explicitly excludes model interpretation. A model would add uncertainty without adding a capability the process lacks.

**6. Agent boundary justification.** Not applicable. Zero agents are proposed.

**9. Authority chain for the consequential operation.** Applying the change is the only consequential operation. Its deterministic control boundary is:

1. **Reasoning:** none by a model. The proposal is the fixed-rule output of a validated form plus the supplied process.
2. **Authorization:** the recorded approval, verified against the named reviewer's identity and the supplied policy. Text that merely claims approval fails this check. A workflow transition is not itself authorization.
3. **Authoritative service:** the supplied service, identity stated by the user but not observed here.
4. **Execution:** the service applies the change only after stage 2 passes.
5. **Audit:** the service's audit result is retained by the process. Store and owner are unknown and not assumed.

The authorization policy is stated as supplied, so the phrase for unspecified policy does not apply. Its content was not observed.

**10. Generic mechanism and product delegation.** Generic mechanism: a durable workflow hosting deterministic activities, with one human wait state and one terminal rejection branch. Product mapping is delegated to the installed maf-architecture-mapping skill and has not been run here.

**11. Rejected alternatives.**

- **All-deterministic:** sufficient for every step, and it is the chosen routing once persisted process control is added.
- **Bounded-AI-only:** insufficient, because a model call cannot enforce the approval and execution protocol and the requirement forbids model interpretation.
- **Agent-everywhere:** could execute the steps but grants discretion the supplied process does not, adding risk at the authorization stage for no gain.
- **Multi-agent:** no security, context, deployment, ownership, model, or specialisation boundary exists to justify even one agent, let alone two.
- **Workflow-only:** sufficient with deterministic activities and no agentic step, and it is the chosen routing.

**12. Decisions, evidence, trade-offs, assumptions, open questions.**

- **Decision:** workflow with deterministic activities. The word "agent" in similar requests describes automation, not adaptive control.
- **Evidence:** the requirement text alone, rung 4. No runtime source was observed.
- **Trade-off:** the design has no flexibility for form shapes or routes outside the supplied process. That rigidity is the point. Introducing a model later would require re-running the bounded-AI challenge.
- **Assumption, labelled:** the recorded approval is durable and attributable to the named reviewer. If it is only an unsigned message, stage 2 cannot be satisfied.
- **Open questions:** the seven items in the fact table's third column, above all approval authentication, wait timeout, apply-call idempotency, and audit retention ownership.
