## Step 2 ΓÇö Responsibility-to-construct mapping

| Responsibility | MAF construct | Architectural reason |
|---|---|---|
| Conversational state across turns within one interaction | **`AgentSession`** | Provides interaction-scoped continuity. It is not durable cross-session memory and should not be treated as a corpus-retrieval mechanism. |
| Retrieval from an external corpus | **`AIContextProvider`**, backed by a search source such as **`TextSearchProvider`** | Keeps retrieved evidence separate from the agentΓÇÖs reasoning and makes corpus-derived context attributable and citable. |
| Optional handoff workflow orchestration | **Workflow graph with a human gate**, using the handoff workflow constructs | Handoff is workflow orchestration: sequencing and transition control remain explicit even though individual agents may be autonomous. The surface reference records **`AgentWorkflowBuilder`** and the handoff builder name; this is not a reason to assume any group-chat builder name. |
| Peer A2A delegation | **Agent-card discovery and task delegation through A2A** | The peer receives a task description rather than a local function call. This is distinct from agent-as-tool consultation and requires a genuine deployment/ownership boundary. |

## Step 3 ΓÇö Runtime and control placement

The hosting choice remains separate from these mechanism mappings: the agent may run in-process or in a separately hosted runtime, but that choice determines session isolation and lifecycle ownership.

For the A2A path, discovery must precede delegation. The peerΓÇÖs ingress contract, protocol version, and wire binding must be checked independently; both sides claiming A2A support does not establish compatibility. The surface reference specifically records JSON-RPC versus HTTP+JSON binding compatibility between hosted peers as **unverified**. Do not delegate until the actual bindings are confirmed compatible.

If the handoff workflow includes authorization, place human authority at the matching layer:

- workflow transition approval ΓåÆ workflow-level gate;
- consequential individual tool invocation ΓåÆ per-call approval;
- acting against downstream systems as the caller ΓåÆ delegated-access consent.

A propagated caller identifier alone does not establish delegated authority.

## Step 4 ΓÇö Assurance

Capture execution evidence independently of the agentΓÇÖs summary: session continuity, retrieved sources, handoff transitions, delegation requests and responses, binding-negotiation results, approvals, and tool outcomes. Evaluate those observations against the specific requirements, then verify that behavior still matches the routing decision. In particular, confirm that peer delegation remains task-level A2A rather than silently becoming a local function-tool call. Re-run these checks after framework, workflow, hosting, or peer-contract changes.
