## Runtime, control, and assurance (steps 3 and 4)

- **Hosting placement is undecided.** The decision does not say whether this agent runs in-process or in a hosted runtime. If hosted, per-session sandbox isolation applies, and the surface reference marks that as one spike's finding, not a standing guarantee.
- **Session lifetime belongs to the hosting layer.** The session construct gives continuity within an interaction, but the design must not assume it outlives the host's idle and reclaim policy.
- **Ingress and binding gate the A2A row.** Delegation is layered on the peer's ingress, and agent-card discovery is a separate step precisely because both sides supporting A2A does not imply a shared binding or protocol version. Discovery and a compatibility check must precede any delegation. Until that check passes, the design cannot assume delegation works.
- **Identity across the peer boundary is open.** If the delegated task must act as the calling user, a propagated identifier is not a grant. That needs an explicit delegated-access consent step, and the decision does not say whether this applies.
- **No human-authority layer is invoked.** None of the four responsibilities names a gate. If handoff is later adopted and a stage needs sign-off, it belongs at the workflow gate of row 8, not the per-call approval of row 9. I am not adding one.
- **Observe:** capture retrieved passages, tool calls, session turn boundaries, any handoff transition, and the A2A task sent and result received, independently of the agent's narration.
- **Evaluate:** check that answers cite retrieved content rather than paraphrase it, that state does not leak across interactions, and that delegated results are attributed to the peer.
- **Verify behaviour:** if the agent never varies its next step, re-route down. If it hands off on every run, the decision has become multi-agent and must go back to the router.
- **Regress:** re-run behavioural checks after any SDK move, given the recorded drift from the old thread type to the session type.
